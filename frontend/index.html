On Earth, hot gas rises and drags fresh air into the flame. In orbit, nothing rises.

Without buoyancy, a flame loses its teardrop shape and feeds only on slow diffusion or on whatever airflow the cabin fans supply. That is why NASA had to fly its fire experiments, and why Earth fire-safety tests don't transfer directly to a spacecraft or a Moon base.

Each place has its own gravity and cabin air. 1gnition checks every indexed run against them, using a tolerance of ±1 point of oxygen and ±5 kPa of pressure.

How 1gnition decides

Rules do the matching and ranking. Every claim carries one of four result states, and the AI only explains what the records say.

  1. Your scenario

    Material, oxygen, pressure, gravity, airflow. Blank fields stay blank.

  2. Two scores, kept apart

    Scientific relevance and environmental similarity, with stated tolerances.

  3. Fair-comparison check

    A pair that changes several conditions at once is never credited to one of them.

  4. A labeled answer

    Published observationExploratory comparisonIndependently evaluated predictionInsufficient evidence

1gnition — Know What the Evidence Supports.
1gnition is a student project for NASA Space Apps 2026. It is a research and education tool, not a NASA product and not a fire-safety certification tool. The physical explanations shown are those of the published authors, chiefly Olson et al. (2023) and Urban et al. (2021); 1gnition did not discover confinement effects or run new burns. Its contribution is a traceable workflow for deciding what the evidence supports.
