# IGNIS | Scientific Lead decision
**15 November 2026 · Published flight evidence**

**Decision.** Demonstrate that two pairs of flames reached the same reported length while spreading at different rates. Then show the much larger I–IV rate difference and the conditions that changed with it. This is a result the team can reproduce today. Reserve the width-by-height hypothesis for a controlled test.

| Wide SIBAL baseline → comparison | Flow in Olson (cm/s) | Spread rate (cm/s) | Change from baseline | Flame / pyrolysis lengths (cm) |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| I-1 → II-5, narrower specimen | 20 | 0.18 → 0.21 | +0.03 (+16.7%) | 4 / 4 in both |

Each percentage uses the first flight as denominator and rounded Table 1 inputs. A rate measures how fast the flame base advances. A length measures how far the flame or pyrolysis zone extends at a given time. The two +0.03 differences do not establish a repeatable width effect. Neither pair holds specimen length, position on the card, atmosphere or flight fixed. I–IV holds width, material and reported inlet speed fixed, but length, oxygen, pressure, humidity and imaging also changed. The evidence supports three observed contrasts. It supplies no within-condition estimate of variability.

**Scientific question.** At a fixed concurrent inlet speed and atmosphere in microgravity, how does reducing full duct height change SIBAL flame-base spread rate at each of two specimen widths?

**Hypothesis to test.** A shallower duct will increase the mean rate more for the wide specimen than for the narrow one. Heated gas around a wide flame has less room to escape sideways, while oxygen can reach a narrow flame from its sides. This predicts a positive difference between the wide and narrow height responses. A zero or reversed interaction would challenge that prediction. The historical flights leave this interaction unidentified.

**What IGNIS adds.** Olson and colleagues already reported and interpreted the confinement association. IGNIS connects each displayed number to its table, plot or archive entry, identifies shared burns across sources, and shows which variables change when an engineer selects a comparison. In the demonstration, switch from flame length to spread rate, open the source conflicts, then show the missing narrow-specimen/shallow-duct SIBAL condition at 20 cm/s. Even adding that cell alone would leave the historical controls unequal. The proposed factorial protocol makes all four cells comparable.
