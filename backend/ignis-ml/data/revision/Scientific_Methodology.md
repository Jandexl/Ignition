# Measurement basis and proposed experiment

## What the measurements represent

Olson Table 1 contains eight concurrent-flow records (seven SIBAL, one cotton). SIBAL is 75% cotton and 25% fiberglass. Pure cotton burns away and can curl, while the fiberglass skeleton remains after the SIBAL flame passes. On flight V the spread-rate fit uses the base trajectory before curling compromised tracking (before 300 mm, under 100 s). Saffire IV and V were short enough to limit evidence for a prolonged length plateau. Treat their tabulated lengths as paper-reported values. The BASS pyrolysis lengths are unavailable. Missing lengths have no numerical value.

Olson §3.3 tracks the base, visible flame tip and pyrolysis front separately. Dark enhanced frames reveal the dim tip. Illuminated frames show the fuel surface and pyrolysis front. Spread rate is a slope in distance versus time. Visible flame length is tip minus base, and pyrolysis length is front minus base. These endpoints can diverge: flight V gives 14 cm and 10 cm. Urban Table 2 reports steady and maximum “size” as different columns. For V, numerical coincidences with those columns do not establish identical endpoint definitions.

## Physical interpretation and limits

- Thermal expansion near the flame diverts flow. For a wide flame in a shallow duct the authors estimate greater core acceleration, which can raise convective heating of unburned fuel. The calculation uses flame dimensions and estimated temperatures. Earlier Saffire flame heights were estimated because side views were unavailable.
- Wall and surviving SIBAL surface boundary layers grow downstream. The slower near-surface layer changes the velocity at the base, while displacement of the outer flow accelerates the core. Equal bulk inlet speeds can therefore produce different velocities and heating at the fuel. Saffire II’s narrow specimens began farther down the card, already in a developed layer.
- A narrow flame can expand laterally and draw oxygen in from both sides. Enhanced edge oxygen transport could sustain faster spread with a smooth approach to the final length. Competing heat loss to a metal holder is especially important for the narrowest BASS fabric. The BASS rates of 0.27 and 0.51 cm/s also correspond to different inlet speeds, holder widths and oxygen levels. A universal width trend cannot be inferred.
- Wide SIBAL showed an early excess pyrolysis length. Wide cotton also overshot despite burning away upstream, weakening an explanation that requires a growing layer along surviving fuel. Olson proposes a post-ignition transient while preheat and pyrolysis regions develop. Their estimated accelerated-flow correlation worked better for the approximately 20 cm/s tests than at 25 cm/s. It supplies a physical rationale, with no independent local velocity validation in this comparison.
- The reported characteristic SIBAL burnout time near 22 s provides a useful secondary check. Pyrolysis length divided by spread rate from the rounded I, II-5, II-6, III and IV entries gives approximately 22.2, 19.0, 19.2, 21.7 and 23.6 s. These derived values reuse the same measurements.

## Comparability

Both width comparisons share SIBAL, concurrent orbital flow, the same published duct and the same tabulated speed within each pair. The narrow samples are 29 × 5 cm and the wide ones 94 × 40.6 cm. The narrow samples were positioned farther downstream. II-5 versus I differs by +0.4 percentage points O₂ and +1 kPa. II-6 versus III differs by +0.9 points and +4 kPa. The article reports 47%, 45%, 46% and 37% relative humidity on flights I, II, III and IV respectively. Batch equality, local flow histories, identical fit windows and per-run measurement errors are unverified. The archive flow disagreements further weaken the 25 cm/s match.

For I → IV, full height changes from 51 to 30.5 cm and sample length from 94 to 50 cm. Oxygen rises 21.7% → 23.1%, pressure falls 102 → 99 kPa, and camera geometry changes. Duct length and width match. The SIBAL flame and pyrolysis lengths each increase by 4.5 cm (2.125-fold). The observed doubling of spread rate does not quantify the contribution of height alone. The 20 and 25 values for the four-run opening comparison are **airflow in cm/s**. They are unsuitable as oxygen labels.

## Controlled factorial protocol

Use fresh SIBAL specimens at 5 and 40.6 cm exposed widths, crossed with 30.5 and 51 cm full duct heights. Use the same 109 × 46 cm duct planform, centered specimen plane, common specimen length and entrance-to-igniter distance in every cell. Select an achievable common length/window in a pilot that allows the flame to pass the ignition transient before end effects. Set a measured concurrent bulk speed of 20 cm/s for all cells, adjusting volumetric flow for cross section. Record the full inlet profile, near-base and core velocities, wall gaps and left/right clearances. Preserve velocity differences produced by the geometry as candidate mechanisms.

Match O₂, pressure, RH, gas and wall temperatures, gravity regime, fuel batch, areal density, thickness, weave orientation, moisture conditioning, mounting tension, holder material/contact and ignition energy per unit exposed width. Match wall optical/thermal properties, entrance development and exhaust conditions. Monitor gas composition and flow through the burn. Begin each test from reset hardware and atmosphere. Document deviations; report failed ignition and extinction separately. Side clearance necessarily changes with width in a fixed duct. The width contrast includes the resulting edge transport.

Synchronize calibrated surface and side cameras, dark and illuminated exposures, fiducials and timestamps. Track the centerline base, tip and pyrolysis front. Fit base position against time over a prespecified common physical window after ignition and before end effects. Set minimum usable duration, allowable curvature and plateau criteria in the pilot. If the criteria fail, report the transient or censored endpoint. Retain the full trajectories, transient peak, time to plateau, and steady lengths. Check a second analyst’s annotations and sensitivity to threshold, calibration and window. Measure local flow with validated, non-perturbing velocimetry if feasible; use surface thermocouples and near-edge oxygen diagnostics to distinguish heat-transfer and oxygen-transport explanations.

Randomize all four configurations within each flight/session block where hardware permits. If height can change only between sessions, randomize height across multiple independent sessions at each level and randomize widths within them; analyze height at the session level. One flight per height would confound flight with height. Begin with at least three separately ignited specimens per cell as a variance pilot (12 burns). Use pilot variance and a prespecified smallest meaningful interaction to size the confirmatory campaign. Frames, tracks and multiple sensors from one burn are repeated measurements of one specimen.

For each width, subtract mean deep-duct rate from mean shallow-duct rate. Subtract the narrow-width contrast from the wide-width contrast. Report the interaction in cm/s with an uncertainty interval based on independent specimens and the randomization blocks. Report all four cell means, spreads, counts, exclusions and height contrasts. A mechanistic interpretation additionally requires local-flow or oxygen evidence. No existing row serves as a held-out test of this protocol.
